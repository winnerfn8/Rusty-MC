    World height: each level's shape from its dimension type

    Vanilla takes each level's build range from its dimension type: min_y and height, multiples of 16, with height >= 16 and min_y + height <= 2032 (DimensionType.MIN_Y = -2032, Y_SIZE = 4064: BlockPos packs Y in 12 bits), plus logical_height (how high portals and chorus fruit reach) and has_skylight. LevelHeightAccessor derives everything else from them. Data packs that reshape the Overworld make it taller: JJThunder's To The Max sets min_y -64, height 2096, the largest top vanilla allows, which is 131 sections against vanilla's 24.

    The model

    chunk::LevelShape is one level's shape: min_y, height, logical_height, sky_light, checked as the dimension type codec checks them (LevelShape::new). LevelShapes holds a world's three.

    • Where it comes from. A local world's shapes are its dimension types, its enabled data packs' over vanilla's, through the level stems that name them (worldgen::overrides::WorldgenOverrides::level_height, read by game/world_loading.rs's level_shapes when the world opens; the game keeps them in Game.level_shapes). A remote client takes a level's shape from the server's minecraft:dimension_type registry (protocol::registry::DimensionType, with its logical height). A type the codec refuses keeps the vanilla shape, with a warning.
    • Where it lives. Each level's World holds its shape, SaveDir::new(root, shape) takes it, and the generator reads the same dimension type for the level's build range (OverworldGenerator::level).
    • The column. LevelShape::column() is the span of sections the level's chunks hold (ColumnLayout): the level's own range, widened to the vanilla Overworld's -64..320. So vanilla's three levels keep the 24 sections they have always had, and a taller Overworld gets its own column while its Nether and End stay at 24. Every Chunk records its own min_y and section count, and the hot accessors read the chunk.
    • No global. The shape travels with the data (World, SaveDir, Chunk, the light and mesh jobs, the renderer's clear_sections(layout), the LAN encoder's set_dimension(shape), the remote ChunkFormat). Test binaries run tests in parallel in one process, so a process-wide height would leak between them.
    Subsystem Reads
    Chunk storage (chunk.rs) the chunk's own min_y and sections
    Light (light.rs) the chunk's sections; the relighter the world's column (LightWorld::layout)
    World, streaming, meshing, renderer World::layout(), the chunk's min_y, the renderer's layout
    Saves (save.rs) SaveDir.shape (sections, yPos, heightmaps, light layers, far tiles)
    Network (lan/, game/remote_chunks.rs) the level's shape on the host, the server's dimension type on the client
    Section caches, generation glue, far terrain, minimap, flat worlds the chunk's column, the generator's level range
    Gameplay (bounds, searches, commands, mobs) World::bottom_y/top_y (the column), World.shape, Game.level_shapes

    Section sets are SectionMasks (256 bits: vanilla's tallest level has 254 sections, light data one more below and above). Uniform sections (one block, one biome, one light value) are one shared allocation per kind (chunk::share_uniform), and the sky-source scan passes over a uniform section light goes through: a tall column is mostly such air.

    A world of data packs

    The game, single player and LAN alike, runs through a host: the integrated server thread of single player reads the world's shapes, encodes each level at its shape (HubConfig.levels) and sends the data of a dimension type the packs changed even to a client that knows the vanilla pack (NetConfig.levels, SyncData::registry_packets), and its client takes the heights from that registry data, as a vanilla client of a vanilla server does.

    Checks

    • Unit tests: a 131-section chunk stores, lights, relights, saves, loads, encodes and random-ticks its whole height; tests/tall_column.rs streams, lights and meshes a 131-section level to its top and remeshes after an edit at y 1800.
    • HORIZON_PARITY_DATAPACKS=tools/fixtures/tall_datapacks python3 tools/test_datapack_worldgen_parity.py generates the fixture's 2096-high Overworld (horizon_tall_test, Rusty Minecraft's own MIT pack, terrain from about y 475 to 1900) with the vanilla oracle, which takes each level's range from its dimension type, and with Rusty Minecraft, and compares every block, biome and block entity.
    • Vanilla worlds are unchanged at every step: chunkgen_bench --snapshot, test_light_parity.py and the save and LAN tests give the same results as before.

    What is left

    • Nether and End at their own height. They keep the vanilla column (-64..320). Holding exactly their 16 sections would save memory and match vanilla's LevelChunk, but Rusty Minecraft's light only spreads inside a chunk's column while vanilla's spreads one section past the level, so it needs that border in the flood fill and the relighter first, checked with test_light_parity.py.
    • The vanilla constants MIN_Y, HEIGHT and SECTIONS remain as the default column and in tests.
    • Far terrain's detail for a camera far above the world uses vanilla's top (lod.rs).
    • Looking down from far above the terrain shows fog: vanilla's render-distance fog uses max(xz, |y|), so terrain more than the render distance below the camera is fogged in vanilla too.
