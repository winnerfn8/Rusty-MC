    Section meshing parity

    tools/test_section_mesh_parity.py compares the vertices Rusty Minecraft's section mesher (crates/horizon-client-render/src/mesher.rs, with mesher/lighting.rs and mesher/fluid.rs) emits with the vertices the pinned client's own renderers emit for the same scenes.

    What is compared

    • Vanilla side: VanillaSectionMeshOracle bootstraps the game, binds the prepared block tags, parses the jar's blockstate definitions and block models, and bakes BlockStateModels through the real ModelDiscovery, UnbakedRoot.bake and FaceBakery path. Every texture becomes a real sprite (its PNG from the jar) alone in a unit atlas, so UVs stay in sprite-local [0,1] space; the five fluid sprites share one strip with gaps so the fluid vertex stream still identifies them. A fixed BlockAndTintGetter serves the scene's block states, per-cell sky/block light, constant biome colours per ColorResolver and the overworld or Nether CardinalLighting. Blocks of one section are visited like SectionCompiler.compile: fluids through FluidRenderer.tesselate, then RenderShape.MODEL blocks through ModelBlockRenderer.tesselateBlock with smooth lighting on, culling on, cutout leaves, BlockColors.createDefault() and state.getSeed(pos).
    • Rusty Minecraft side: crates/horizon-client/examples/probe/section_mesh_fixture.rs loads the prepared resources (Assets, BlockFacts, Biomes, load_visibility, MeshTables), builds the same blocks and light in real client Chunks (a 3x3 chunk region), sets every biome's colours to the scene constants (or, for the biome-blend scenes, keeps the real biome definitions and fills the chunks' quart biome arrays) and runs Mesher::mesh.
    • Comparison: quads are matched as multisets per scene, keyed by sprite, render-layer class (translucent vs. solid/cutout) and geometry rounded to 1/1000 after binary32 normalisation, with a winding-preserving rotation. Matched quads compare per vertex: positions and UVs within 1e-4, ARGB colour and light coordinates (sky and block, 1/16 steps) exactly.
    • Per-state facts: a second gate compares, for all 35,723 states, the facts the Rust tables derive from prepared resources with the client's accessors: getShadeBrightness, emissiveRendering, isSolid, isLightPermeable, isSolidRender, isCollisionShapeFullBlock, light emission, the water overlay classes (HalfTransparentBlock/LeavesBlock), tint source counts and getOffset at five columns.

    The corpus (112 sections, ~1.0M quads, ~206k of them fluid quads) has full cubes in open air, corners, cavities and overhangs; slabs, stairs, walls, fences, panes, bars, torches, flowers and grasses with XZ/XYZ offsets, tall plants, tinted blocks (grass, leaves, stems, redstone, lily pad, petals, leaf litter, cauldrons), snow layers, carpets, farmland, paths, hoppers, anvils, chains, lanterns, dripleaf, dripstone; water and lava sources, flowing levels, falling columns, waterlogged blocks, water against glass/leaves/slabs/ice; section edges with neighbours in other chunks and sections; a cave with emissive and light-emitting blocks (magma, glowstone, sea lantern, active sculk sensor, glow lichen); Nether shading; every block's default state; every RenderShape.MODEL state of the registry on a sparse grid; and dense random scenes mixing all model states with fluids under propagated or random light.

    Biome tint blending is compared against the client's real ClientLevel.calculateBlockTint: the oracle builds a BiomeManager (the eight-corner zoom with a random hashed seed) over a fixed noise-biome map of real Biome objects from the registry bootstrap (colormap sampling, colour overrides, the dark-forest and swamp grass modifiers), initialises GrassColor/FoliageColor/DryFoliageColor from the jar's colormaps, and averages the resolver over the blend radius at each block's own height. The biome_blend_r* scenes (radius 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7) put tinted blocks (grass, leaves, water, leaf litter, cane, ferns, vines) at four heights on every column of a Voronoi patchwork of 16 common biomes that shifts with height; the biome_each_* scenes cover all 67 biomes. Option scenes (options_*) cover flat lighting (ao off), opaque leaves (cutoutLeaves off, which SectionCompiler draws in the solid layer and LeavesBlock.skipRendering joins) and both together.

    With biomeBlendRadius 0 the client takes the resolver's colour as is. Biome colours given as plain RGB (all water colours, most grass/foliage overrides) have alpha 0 there, so the client's tinted vertices come out fully transparent, which the terrain shader would discard. Rusty Minecraft keeps them opaque and the gate compares RGB for radius-0 scenes.

    Running

    python3 tools/test_section_mesh_parity.py            # full corpus
python3 tools/test_section_mesh_parity.py --quick    # skip the all-states catalogue
python3 tools/test_section_mesh_parity.py --strict   # exit 1 on any difference
python3 tools/test_section_mesh_parity.py --only fluids

    It needs the pinned jars (tools/lab.py prepare) and prepared client resources (cargo run --release -p horizon-prepare -- --prepare). Outputs, logs and report.json go to target/section-mesh-parity/; nothing is written under .local/. The fixture is built with --profile fast and reports its meshing time.

    Results

    Before the port (commit 2940b5a, first 18 scenes): 25,391 of 55,017 vanilla quads identical (46%); 7,685 fluid quads missing, 9,231 extra quads, and light/colour differences on most of the rest.

    After: all 841,775 quads of the 94 scenes match and are identical, and all per-state facts match. Meshing cost is unchanged within noise: the fixture meshes the 94 (dense) corpus sections in about 3 ms each with both the old and the ported mesher (--profile fast, loaded 24-thread desktop).

    What was ported to get there:

    • BlockModelLighter: quad shape (faceCubic/facePartial), the corner, diagonal and centre samples (diagonals only through light-permeable blocks, the centre from the neighbour unless it is solid-render), shade brightness overrides, emissive full-bright samples, smoothBlend (zero samples take the centre, sky-less samples borrow its sky, only when the centre is above 2), the partial-face weights with smoothWeightedBlend's int narrowing, and the vertex remap. The padded section border grew to two blocks because cubic faces sample two blocks out along their normal.
    • ModelBlockRenderer: flat lighting for light-emitting blocks or when the first model part disables AO (culled quads read light from the cull neighbour, others from the neighbour only when flush), ARGB.gray/ scaleRGB/multiply integer arithmetic, cardinal shading including NETHER, shade-direction overrides, per-element light emission, tint source counts (layer indices past the list are untinted; water and bubble columns are untinted in the world) and getOffset for XZ/XYZ blocks.
    • FluidRenderer: face culling against the neighbour's occlusion face shape for the fluid box height, self occlusion, isSolid corner heights, the weighted corner averages in vanilla order, the 0.001 insets (also feeding the side faces), FlowingFluid.getFlow with Mth.atan2 and the sine table, the water overlay beside half-transparent and leaf blocks, back faces, max(self, above) light, bottom light from below, and cardinal shading.
    • UnbakedCuboidGeometry.bake: elements flat along an axis draw no faces across it (bake_block_model in horizon-assets; the raw bake_model_transformed used by the model-baking gate is unchanged).

    Exported facts used: block-behaviours.json (class chains, isSolid, full face sturdiness), gameplay-facts.json (offset flag, emission, shapes), the #minecraft:blocks_fluid_flow tag and the occlusion faces already loaded for face visibility. Offset types and maximum offsets, shade-brightness and emissive overrides are keyed by class or block name and checked by the facts gate.

    What remains different or untested

    • Changing biomeBlendRadius, ao or cutoutLeaves in the options does not remesh resident sections; new and edited sections use the new values.
    • Model variant seeds: DoublePlantBlock, DoorBlock and AbstractBedBlock override getSeed; Rusty Minecraft seeds every block from its own position. No vanilla block among those has weighted variants, so output is unaffected.
    • Opaque leaves: the vertex layer carries a flag (OPAQUE_LAYER) that makes the terrain shader ignore the texture's alpha; the shader side is not compared (GPU pixels).
    • Layers: vanilla's solid and cutout layers are compared as one class because Rusty Minecraft draws both in its opaque pass.
    • Not compared: block entities and their renderers, animated sprite frames, translucent sorting, chunk-border light outside loaded chunks and GPU pixels.
    From docs/parity/meshing.md. Rusty Minecraft is a fork of Horizon (MIT).
