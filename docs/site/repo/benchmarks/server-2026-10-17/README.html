    Dedicated server against vanilla 26.3, with bots (2026-10-17)

    horizon-client --dedicated (run by horizon-server) against the pinned vanilla 26.3 server.jar, on main at 7bbbf6e9 (in release v2026.10.17.17). That build has the movement checks and chunk pacing of #586, the equal-work method of #670, and the server performance PRs #701–#707 and #713.

    This replaces the data first published here, which came from a Mac mini overloaded by other work (load 55–141 on 14 threads). An earlier run of this method, on main at 002f14ce, is kept in earlier-002f14ce/ and compared at the end.

    Setup

    • Server machine: ThinkPad, Intel i7-7500U (2 cores, 4 threads), 32 GB, NixOS. Nothing else heavy ran on it: its load average was 0.4 when idle. Both servers ran under nice -n 19.
    • Bots: horizon-bots on another machine (Mac mini M4 Pro), over the tailnet. That machine was shared with other work (load 36–170 on 14 threads, mean 78). The bots need little CPU, but a busy bot host can answer later. Both servers saw the same conditions, since they took turns at each bot count.
    • Horizon: release build of 7bbbf6e9 (horizon-server, horizon-client).
    • Vanilla: the pinned 26.3 server.jar on OpenJDK 25.0.4.1, with -Xms4g -Xmx4g.
    • Properties:
      • level-seed=12345, view and simulation distance 10, creative (forced);
      • online-mode=false, difficulty=easy, max-players=200;
      • default game rules on both (the locator bar is on).
    • Each run: a fresh server, bots joining one per second, a 60 s warm-up, then 180 s measured.
    • Commands (on the server machine):
      python3 tools/server_bench.py --servers horizon,vanilla --nice 19 --warmup 60 --seconds 180 \
    --bots-via "ssh <bot host>" --bots-bin <horizon-bots> --connect <server address> \
    --bots 1,10,25,50,100 --behaviour walk,explore
python3 tools/server_bench.py ...same... --bots 1,10,25,50 --behaviour explore --explore-speed 0.8
    • Columns:
      • MSPT (mean) and P95 come from /tick query, in ms. TPS is the tick rate.
      • RSS is the mean over the run. CPU is the whole process tree, as a percentage of one core.
      • Chunks/s is what the bots received. New chunks/s counts each chunk once per bot.
      • Pulled back counts the times the server moved a bot back (vanilla's "moved too quickly" and similar).
      • Load is the mean load average during that run: the server machine (which includes the server's own threads) and the bot host.

    What it shows

    • Same work, 1 to 50 players: Horizon's mean tick is 44–81 % shorter than vanilla's, its 95th percentile is lower, and its process uses 32–63 % of vanilla's CPU.
    • Walking, 1 to 50 players: Horizon's mean tick is shorter at every count (4.0 against 18.2 ms with 1 bot, 22.7 against 34.3 ms with 50).
    • Where vanilla wins:
      • With 25 and 50 walking bots, vanilla's 95th percentile is lower (28.2 against 31.1 ms, 55.6 against 60.2 ms). Horizon's slow ticks are spikier.
      • In the full-speed explore pass, vanilla's mean tick is shorter with 10 and 25 bots (16.9 against 21.3 ms, 24.9 against 25.8 ms), while Horizon sends 1.6–1.8 times as many new chunks.
      • With 50 walking bots, Horizon makes the server machine busier (load 14.7 against 9.5). Its connection writer threads compress every packet (below).
    • 100 players: Horizon keeps 15.8 TPS walking and 19.0 TPS exploring at full speed. Vanilla's watchdog stopped it in both.
    • Memory: Horizon used 0.76–0.92 GB in every walk and equal-work run, and 1.36 GB at most when 100 bots explored at full speed. Vanilla used 3.4–4.0 GB with its 4 GB heap.
    • Start: Horizon's fresh world was ready in 9.2–9.9 s, vanilla's in 14.9–17.2 s.

    Where Horizon's time goes (50 walking bots)

    perf record -e cycles:u --call-graph lbr on the engine for 30 s, during a separate 50-bot walk run on the same build and machine (MSPT 21.7 ms, 178 % CPU). Shares of all the engine's CPU samples:

    Thread Share Mostly
    Connection writers (horizon-lan-out) 55 % 53 % in miniz_oxide deflate, from horizon_protocol::frame::encode
    World generation workers 33 % noise, surface rules, aquifers
    Server thread (the tick) 12 % see below

    Inside the tick:

    • the locator bar (Waypoints::tick): a third of the tick;
    • Hub::tick: a quarter;
    • block entities, creatures, chunk encoding (ChunkEncoder::encode_in) and Streaming::update: 7–10 % each;
    • SipHash of u128 keys (the default HashMap hasher): about 10 % of the tick.

    The next biggest cost is compressing packets. Every chunk packet is compressed again for each player who gets it, with flate2's default miniz_oxide backend at level 6. On this 4-thread CPU that competes with the tick and with world generation. Two fixes, which can be combined:

    • the zlib-rs backend for flate2: 2.2–10 times faster than miniz_oxide at level 6 in a quick test, with output about 3 % larger;
    • compressing a chunk packet once and sending the same bytes to every player who needs it.
