    Horizon's server against vanilla 26.3, Paper and Fabric + Lithium: mixed bots, equal work (2026-10-19)

    This is the same load as ../server-equal-mixed-2026-10-18/, now with two more servers: Paper, and vanilla on Fabric with Lithium. It also uses a newer Horizon release (v2026.10.19). That release spawns mobs around every player (#950) and ticks blocks around every player (#931), as vanilla does. The 2026-10-18 build did neither for players away from spawn, so it had less work to do than vanilla, and its tick times there were lower than they would have been with equal work. These numbers replace them.

    Summary

    • The work was equal at every count, on all four servers. Chunks sent a second and first-time chunks a second were within 4 % of each other, and the walking bots got about the same chunks each (6050 to 6510). Every bot joined and none was dropped. Vanilla's 50-bot server is the exception: its watchdog stopped it.
    • Memory: Horizon used 0.72 GB with one player and 1.11 GB with 50. The three Java servers used 3.4 to 4.5 GB (a 4 GB heap each), so Horizon was 3.8 to 4.8 times smaller at every count.
    • Start: 8.2 to 8.5 s to Done for Horizon, against 14.7 to 15.5 s for vanilla, 16.6 to 17.6 s for Fabric + Lithium and 19.0 to 22.4 s for Paper.
    • CPU (100 % is one of the machine's 4 threads): from 10 players up, Horizon used the least: 143 % at 50, against Paper's 210 % and Fabric's 236 %. With one player Paper used less (32 % against 46 %).
    • Average tick (MSPT), where Horizon loses:
      • With 1 player, Paper (10.3 ms) and Fabric + Lithium (12.5) were faster than Horizon (13.7). Vanilla was slowest (17.3).
      • With 10, Fabric (20.2) and Paper (20.6) were ahead of Horizon (22.9), which tied vanilla (22.9).
      • With 25, Paper (28.3) was ahead of Horizon (30.9), and Horizon was ahead of Fabric (31.4) and vanilla (37.4).
      • With 50, Paper (46.9) and Horizon (47.8) were close. Fabric + Lithium fell to 17.2 TPS (58.3 ms), and vanilla's watchdog stopped its server.
    • Slow ticks, where Horizon wins: Horizon's P99 was the lowest from 10 players up (72.0 ms at 25 and 73.2 at 50, against Paper's 90.4 and 109.7). Its worst 5-second P99 was the lowest at every count (133 ms at 50, against 213 for Paper and 888 for Fabric).

    So on this 4-thread laptop CPU, Horizon is not yet the fastest on average tick time. Paper is ahead at every count: Horizon's average tick is a third longer than Paper's with one player and 2 % longer with 50. Fabric + Lithium is ahead with 1 and 10 players. Horizon is ahead on memory, start time, CPU from 10 players up, and the steadiness of its ticks (P99). Its average tick is what perf-server-11 is working on.

    Equal work

    server_bench.py's work table compares, at each bot count, every server's chunks sent a second and first-time chunks a second with the busiest server's. The work is equal when both are within 10 % (or within a chunk a second when there are few).

    Bots Chunks/s sent, first-time (Horizon; vanilla; Paper; Fabric + Lithium) Equal
    1 9.94, 0.01; 9.99, 0.00; 9.81, 0.02; 9.98, 0.00 yes
    10 61.21, 1.78; 61.63, 1.84; 60.76, 1.85; 61.65, 1.84 yes
    25 155.81, 5.78; 158.52, 5.71; 156.35, 5.72; 158.89, 5.75 yes
    50 302.68, 11.02; (stopped); 310.51, 11.16; 311.52, 11.28 yes

    Horizon pulled no bot back for moving too quickly (moved too quickly: 0 at every count). The Java servers logged 0 to 46 such lines a run. All four sent position corrections ("Pulled back") at similar rates.

    Method

    tools/server_bench.py ran each configuration on a fresh server:

    • the same server.properties on all four: seed 12345, view and simulation distance 10, offline, creative, difficulty easy, sync-chunk-writes=true, white-list off;
    • gamerule respawn_radius 0, and otherwise each server's default game rules;
    • the server waits for Done, the bots join one a second, warm up for 300 seconds and are measured for 300, with /tick query every 5 seconds and RSS and CPU every second.

    The bots are horizon-bots --behaviour mixed: 60 % walk, 20 % explore and 20 % idle, each choice deterministic per bot. All are creative and fly 24 blocks above spawn. walk flies to random points within 48 blocks at 5.6 blocks a second, explore flies straight out at 0.8 blocks a second (--explore-speed 0.8), and idle hovers.

    Machine. The ThinkPad: Intel Core i7-7500U (2 cores, 4 threads), 32 GB, NixOS. The desktop was meant to run it but didn't wake (~/fleet/power wake desktop timed out twice). No other session ran on the ThinkPad (load.txt, a sample a minute: 0 other sessions throughout). The benchmark ran under nice -n 19 and ionice -c3. The bots ran on the Mac mini (M4 Pro, shared with other work at 51 to 98 % CPU, 82 % on average) and reached the ThinkPad over the tailnet.

    What ran.

    • Horizon: release v2026.10.19 (07f2913d), the server archive horizon-server-linux-x86_64.tar.gz installed with install.sh. horizon-server ran its horizon-engine with game files prepared from the owner's own Minecraft.
    • Vanilla: the pinned 26.3 server jar (the owner's own copy) on OpenJDK 25.0.4.1, -Xms4g -Xmx4g.
    • Paper: 26.3 build 205 (26.3-205-main@a90108f), from PaperMC's download API, checked against its SHA-256. It ran with Paper's defaults except connection-throttle: -1 in bukkit.yml. Every bot connects from one address, and the default throttle (4 s) refused all but the first. Paperclip patched the pinned server jar from its cache. Same Java and heap.
    • Fabric + Lithium: the pinned server jar on Fabric loader 0.19.5 (tools/benchmark-deps/fabric.lock.json) with Lithium mc26.3-0.26.2-fabric (tools/benchmark-deps/mods.lock.json, checked against its SHA-512). Lithium needs no Fabric API. Same Java and heap.

    The Paper and Fabric jars are dev-only downloads into .local/server-bench/jars and are never committed or shipped.

    How it ran. The first pass ran all four servers at 1 and 10 bots, but its Paper 10-bot run hit the connection throttle (1 of 10 bots joined), and it was stopped after that. The 1-bot and 10-bot lines for Horizon, vanilla and Fabric come from that pass, along with Paper's 1-bot line. A second pass with the throttle off ran all four at 25 and 50 bots, then Paper at 10. All runs were on the same machine on the same morning (02:57 to 06:02 UTC). runs.jsonl holds the 16 run lines used here. report-25-50.json and report-paper-10.json are the second pass's reports. The first pass, stopped early, wrote no report. Its lines come from its output.

    Reproduce

    curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/horizon-rust/horizon/main/packaging/server/install.sh | sh
horizon-prepare --prepare --server-jar /path/to/server.jar      # your own copy
cargo build --release -p horizon-bots                            # on the machine that runs the bots
# in a checkout whose .local/minecraft holds the pinned vanilla jars (tools/lab.py prepare):
HORIZON_BENCH_BIN=~/.local/share/horizon/current \
  python3 tools/server_bench.py --servers horizon,vanilla,paper,fabric --bots 1,10,25,50 --behaviour mixed \
    --explore-speed 0.8 --warmup 300 --nice 19 \
    --bots-via 'ssh <bots machine>' --bots-bin /path/to/horizon-bots --connect <this machine's address> \
    --out four-way.json
